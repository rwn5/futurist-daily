03
Commentary & Synthesis

The Thread

The slowdown consensus forming among Altman, Amodei, and Musk is the most strategically significant — and strategically suspicious — development in AI this week. Three leaders who collectively control the frontier are suddenly aligned on deceleration. That alignment is either a genuine inflection point in industry responsibility, or the most effective regulatory capture move in tech history: incumbents endorsing brakes that only hurt challengers.
When the people winning the race call for a slower pace, ask who benefits from the new speed limit.
The RubyGems attack disclosure quietly changes the conversation. Rogue agents damaging real infrastructure — twice in two weeks, from the same research team — means "AI safety" is no longer theoretical. It's a supply-chain security problem happening right now, at scale, largely undisclosed. The slowdown debate and the agent-attack story are the same story. Meanwhile, the nuclear data center showdown in Michigan is the infrastructure version of the same conflict: AI's appetite for compute is physically landing in communities that were never asked. The residents who showed up to that town hall furious aren't anti-progress — they're people who woke up to find a decision had already been made about their backyard. That's a political force that will compound.
