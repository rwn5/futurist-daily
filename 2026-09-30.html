As agentic systems proliferate, the attack surface explodes — and sandboxing is one of the few unsexy but critical infrastructure pieces nobody wants to build first.

Commentary & Synthesis

The Thread

The theme running through DevDay 2026 isn't models — it's memory and persistence. Dots gives ChatGPT a continuous identity across your life. Robinhood's trading agent runs 24/7 without your input. Engineers are pointing frontier LLMs at steering wheels. The shift from "AI as tool you invoke" to "AI as agent that acts" is no longer a forecast — it's the product roadmap.
The graveyard of OpenAI platform bets is getting crowded, and developers building deep integrations on any single layer should take note.
The surveillance story and the red team findings belong in the same sentence. Flock cameras are getting facial recognition from a competitor who says it's inevitable, while Anthropic's own testers confirm their models can now exploit binary vulnerabilities in roughly one in twenty attempts. Neither number sounds alarming in isolation. Together they describe a world where the infrastructure of surveillance and the capability for autonomous exploitation are both maturing on the same timeline — faster than policy, faster than security posture, and certainly faster than public debate. Markets are off — S&P down 0.77%, Nasdaq dropping 0.92%, Bitcoin sliding to $82K — and yet OpenAI is raising at $1.4 trillion. The divergence is worth watching. Either the AI trade is decoupling from broader risk-off sentiment, or it's the last bubble standing. At a $1.4 trillion private valuation with no IPO in sight, someone is going to be right about that question, and the answer will matter enormously.
