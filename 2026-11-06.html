03
Commentary & Synthesis

The Thread

Two stories this week expose a deepening tension at the heart of the AI industry: the race to capture data and the growing resistance to that capture. Meta's forced pause on its AI training terms isn't just a PR stumble — it's evidence that the implicit social contract around data use is being renegotiated in real time, with users and regulators finally holding some cards. The backlash was fast, global, and effective. That's new.

The bottleneck isn't intelligence. It's the messy, analog world that intelligence has to operate in.
The more important structural story is infrastructure fragility. AI data centers are straining diesel supply chains, triggering political opposition from Texas to Brussels, and creating single points of failure that the freight and energy markets are only beginning to price in. When a governor-elect's first major policy stance is a moratorium on compute construction, the "endless growth" narrative for hyperscalers deserves serious scrutiny — independent of what any AI model can actually do.

Meanwhile, the software layer is being quietly rewritten. SaaS as a pricing model is cracking, document parsing remains a stubborn unsolved problem blocking real enterprise deployment, and cybersecurity assumptions built for human-speed attacks are already obsolete. The a16z cybersecurity conversation and the Reducto PDF argument point to the same underlying truth: the gap between what AI demos can do and what AI can reliably do in production is still vast, and closing it is where the next wave of real company-building happens.
