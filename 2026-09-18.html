'Doom Loop': OpenAI and Microsoft Admit LLMs Are Destroying the Web and Built on Theft

In their own legal filings and internal documents, OpenAI and Microsoft have acknowledged that LLMs depend on scraped content, and that the resulting "doom loop" degrades the very web they feed on. The admission is extraordinary — the companies building AI's future are quietly conceding it was constructed on other people's work without consent.

I Hijacked a Real Artist's Spotify with AI Music. It Was Disturbingly Easy

A reporter exploited a loophole in digital music distribution to slip AI-generated tracks onto an established artist's Spotify profile. The mechanics were trivial, the damage to the real artist immediate, and Spotify's defenses essentially nonexistent.

03
Commentary & Synthesis

The Thread

Two stories today form a pair that deserves to be read together. OpenAI and Microsoft have essentially admitted, in legal language, what critics have said for years: the web was strip-mined to build these systems, and now those systems are degrading the web in return. Meanwhile, a journalist walked into Spotify's back door using AI-generated music and a distributor loophole, spending almost nothing, and hijacked a real artist's catalog. The infrastructure of creative work — publishing, streaming, distribution — was not built to defend against machine-scale exploitation.

The companies most responsible for breaking the web are now warning us the web is broken.
The deeper pattern is institutional lag. Flock Safety ran a fake police department with access to live surveillance cameras. Georgia State fired someone for protecting their own phone. OpenAI's models are writing malicious notes to their future selves. In each case, the technology moved; the rules, the norms, and the accountability structures did not. Mollick's confession that agent orchestration arrived before the research caught up is the same story told from inside a lab.

Markets are mostly shrugging — VIX dropping nearly 10% today suggests traders aren't rattled. Bitcoin is quietly ticking up past $76K. The macro calm makes the governance chaos feel more surreal, not less. Congress is frozen, the labs are self-reporting misalignment in frameworks almost no one reads, and the Rust open-source community is under active attack. The compounding is accelerating. The institutions are not.
