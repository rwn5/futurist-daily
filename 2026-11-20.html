404 MEDIA

ICE Is Paying AT&T to Dig Through a Mountain of Call Records

An internal ICE document reveals the agency is purchasing access to AT&T's raw call data and analytical services — a program experts say may sweep up trillions of records. Lawmakers are calling it mass surveillance by another name, and it's happening without broad public awareness.

404 MEDIA

Black Neighborhoods Are Twice as Likely To House a Data Center, Study Finds

A new study maps the AI infrastructure buildout onto historical redlining patterns with striking results: majority-Hispanic neighborhoods face 2.6x the concentration, majority-Asian neighborhoods 4.2x. The costs of powering the AI economy are being distributed anything but evenly.

SIMON WILLISON

Token Fraud Is Here and It's BAD

Claire Vo of ChatPRD documents a surge in AI token fraud hitting real products in production. As AI platforms scale, the attack surface for credential abuse and token theft is growing faster than defenses — a problem the industry has largely ignored until now.

SIMON WILLISON

PostgreSQL 19 Released

Postgres shipped two months late this year — and AI is to blame. The team had to address a flood of issues driven by the explosion of AI-generated code hitting production databases. The release still landed 200+ bugfixes, while AI agents rewrote 185,000 lines of C into 340,000 lines of Rust nearby.

Cathie Wood's Ark Invest Piled Into Anthropic IPO, Buying $90 Million Worth of Shares

Ark committed $90M to Anthropic's historic $81.34B Nasdaq debut — the largest IPO ever. Crypto futures markets are already pricing the stock down ~5% from its first-day close, suggesting early euphoria may be cooling fast.

To Catch a Ransomware Scammer, FBI Posed as Both Victim and Attacker

The FBI ran a double-sided sting — impersonating both the ransomware victim and the threat actors — to catch an alleged fraud scheme in progress. It's a glimpse into how federal investigators are getting creative as ransomware operations grow more sophisticated.

Commentary & Synthesis

The Thread

Two stories this week deserve to be read together: ICE paying AT&T for access to a surveillance dragnet covering potentially trillions of call records, and a study showing data centers are disproportionately sited in minority neighborhoods. The infrastructure of AI — both the compute layer and the intelligence layer — is being built on and aimed at communities with the least political power to push back.

"The costs of powering the AI economy are being distributed anything but evenly — and so is the scrutiny."
Meanwhile, Anthropic's IPO at $81 billion is the most clarifying event in AI's short public history. A company that opened its S-1 with language about civilizational risk just became one of the most valuable tech listings ever. Ark bought $90M worth. Goldman underwrote it. The market has decided that "we might be building something dangerous" is not a reason to pass — it's a reason to get in early. Crypto futures are already pricing a 5% haircut from day one, which suggests the smart money is treating this like any other momentum trade. The Gemini Neon signal from Demis Hassabis is worth watching. Google DeepMind dropped a model that's reportedly outperforming Anthropic's Fable 5.2 on the same week Claude's parent company debuted on Nasdaq. The timing is not accidental. The race for frontier model dominance is now also a race for public market narrative — and DeepMind just reminded everyone they're still very much in it.
