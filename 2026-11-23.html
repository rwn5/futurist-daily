Commentary & Synthesis

The Thread

Two stories above everything else this week: Jensen Huang's ouster from Nvidia and the competing Riemann Hypothesis proof claims — one human, one AI-assisted. They are more connected than they appear. Both are about who gets credit when the lines of authorship blur, and what happens to institutions built around singular genius when that genius becomes distributed or replaceable.

"I believe I have, working with Claude, discovered a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis." — Levent Alpöge
The Nvidia situation is the cleaner signal. Markets are down hard — Nasdaq off 5.29%, VIX spiking to 35.89 — and the AI trade is unwinding in real time. Jensen was the face of that trade. Trump publicly lobbying for his reinstatement tells you everything: Nvidia isn't just a chip company anymore, it's a geopolitical asset, and no one in Washington wants that asset rudderless while Beijing watches.

Meanwhile, Google's quiet sweep of every major benchmark with Gemini 4 Neon deserves more attention than it's getting. The model discourse spent two years treating OpenAI and Anthropic as the axis of competition. Google held the leaderboard top three simultaneously this week. Anthropic just kicked off its IPO roadshow into a crater of a market. The timing is brutal and probably not accidental.

And then there's the Riemann story — two simultaneous claimed proofs of one of mathematics' greatest unsolved problems, one human and one AI-assisted, both unverified, both circulating in the same week. Even if both collapse under peer review, the moment itself is the signal. The question is no longer whether AI can do serious intellectual work. It's whether we'll know how to attribute it when it does.
