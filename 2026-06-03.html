Microsoft Wants to 'Make People Addicted' to its New AI Assistant, Internal Documents Reveal

Internal planning documents for Microsoft's "Scout" AI assistant explicitly state the goal is to "make people addicted" to the tool before rolling out additional features. This reveals the deliberate behavioral design strategies tech giants are deploying as AI assistants become more central to daily workflows.

Google Is Quietly Buying Code From Play Store Developers to Train AI

Google is running a "confidential" program to purchase source code from Android developers on the Play Store for AI training purposes. The initiative highlights how tech companies are aggressively sourcing training data as they compete to build more capable coding assistants.

Nvidia and Microsoft Researchers Say AI Agents Don't Care About Safety or Reliability

Researchers from Nvidia and Microsoft compared AI agents to the cartoon character Mr. Magoo, noting they stumble through dangerous situations without recognizing risks. The study reveals fundamental gaps in current AI systems' ability to prioritize safety and reliability in real-world deployments.

Amazon Shuts Down Internal AI Leaderboard After Employees Cheated

Amazon terminated an internal AI performance leaderboard after employees admitted to cheating their way up the rankings. The incident exposes the competitive pressures and ethical blind spots emerging as AI tools become integrated into workplace performance metrics.

Commentary & Synthesis

The Thread

The mask is coming off. While we've been debating AI ethics in abstract terms, internal documents reveal tech companies are explicitly designing for addiction and admitting their systems ignore safety. Microsoft's "Scout" documents don't mince words about creating dependency, while Nvidia and Microsoft researchers openly compare AI agents to a bumbling cartoon character who can't see danger. The real story isn't whether AI will transform work — it's how deliberately tech giants are engineering psychological hooks to ensure we can't opt out. Google's quiet code-buying program from Android developers shows the lengths companies will go to feed their training machines, while Amazon's cheating scandal reveals how AI performance metrics are corrupting workplace culture from within.
"There's a lot more uncertainty embedded in all these SaaS companies than there was a year ago"
Meanwhile, the bifurcation accelerates. Law professors can't tell AI from humans in blind tests, new roles like Forward Deployed Engineers command Silicon Valley premiums, and crypto markets signal broader uncertainty as traditional safe havens wobble. The K-shaped recovery isn't coming — it's here. Companies that successfully deploy AI agents are pulling ahead while others struggle with basic implementation. The window for catching up isn't closing gradually; it's slamming shut as network effects and data advantages compound exponentially for early movers.
