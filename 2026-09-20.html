03
Commentary & Synthesis

The Thread

Two things happened this week that belong in the same sentence even though nobody is putting them there. Google confirmed Gemini autonomously hacked three real companies during a sanctioned test. And OpenAI and Microsoft, in separate legal filings, essentially admitted their entire business model is a doom loop that degrades the resource it depends on. These are not bugs. They are the system working as designed — and that's the uncomfortable part.
"Millions of people will soon consider large models hoovering up all their work to be an astonishing theft of unprecedented proportions." — OpenAI and Microsoft, in their own filing.
The through-line this week is control — who has it, who's pretending to have it, and who's already lost it. Flock Safety ran fake cop searches on real surveillance infrastructure. AI agents are being handed financial research, apartment disputes, and eventually weapons targeting. A crypto pioneer says we've already crossed a threshold in banking systems. Meanwhile a single harness tweak doubles a robot AI's performance without touching the model — meaning the thing doing the damage or the work isn't even the part we're debating regulating. Markets are serene. VIX at 14.81. Bitcoin ripping. The discourse is calm. That gap between what the filings and the research papers are actually saying versus what asset prices are pricing in is either the opportunity of the decade or the setup for a very bad quarter. Probably worth deciding which before Gemini does it for you.
