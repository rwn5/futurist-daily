A16z dropped the stat of the week: AI agents now consume nearly five times the tokens that human users do, a figure that has grown 14x since February. This isn't a usage trend — it's a structural shift in who (or what) the internet's compute is actually serving. The infrastructure assumptions of cloud pricing, API design, and even security are built for humans. That era is ending.

Ethan Mollick called out a real methodological rot in AI research: papers measuring AI's impact using outdated models, without adequately flagging the limitation. In a field where a three-month-old benchmark can be irrelevant, publishing results from older models without clear caveats is closer to misinformation than science.

Commentary & Synthesis

The Thread

The week's data tells a single coherent story: AI is scaling past the point where humans are the primary actor, user, or even the primary beneficiary. Agents are burning 5x the compute of people. Linus Torvalds is outsourcing kernel debugging. Legal departments are adopting Codex at 108x growth rates. The transition from "AI as tool" to "AI as workforce" is no longer a forecast — it's the current state.
"Humans are the minority user of AI. Agents burn nearly 5x the tokens people do, up 14x since February."
The Anthropic story is the sharpest contradiction in the space right now. Revenue is exploding — $65B annualized — yet its best model can't win the consumer market against cheaper rivals. This is what commoditization looks like from the inside: the top of the capability curve generates prestige but not necessarily users, while the middle of the market captures volume. Anthropic's business is fine. Its model dominance is not guaranteed. The subtlefakes story and the nuclear data center moratorium sit at opposite ends of AI's collateral damage spectrum — one is an intimate abuse problem, the other is a geopolitical infrastructure fight — but both are local governments and individuals pushing back with whatever tools they have. Neither is winning cleanly. That's the political texture of the next few years: not a grand reckoning, just a thousand small friction points that compound.
