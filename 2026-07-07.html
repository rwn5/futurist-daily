Commentary & Synthesis

The Thread

Two stories today that look unrelated are telling the same story. Companies are throttling employee AI usage because costs are exploding, while Claude Fable 5 — arguably the most capable model available — is simultaneously being pulled from subscription plans. The economics of frontier AI are broken in both directions: too expensive for enterprises to run freely, and too strategically sensitive for governments to leave unrestricted.

The tokenpocalypse isn't a bug. It's the business model colliding with reality.
The deeper pattern is a capability-cost squeeze that nobody budgeted for. Enterprises bought into AI productivity promises before anyone stress-tested what "every employee using frontier models all day" actually costs at scale. Tencent's Hy3 — 295 billion parameters, Apache 2.0, free to run — lands directly into this gap. If you can self-host a model that competes with GPT-5 class systems, the calculus on paying per-token changes entirely. Meanwhile, the MTurk collapse closes a loop that started a decade ago. Humans trained the models that replaced the humans doing the training. Governance attacks on BONK — spending $4M to steal $20M — show the same dynamic in crypto: systems optimized for openness get exploited by actors who simply read the rules more carefully than the designers did. The week's signal isn't any single story. It's that every system built on cheap assumptions is getting a stress test at once.
