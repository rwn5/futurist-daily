Google Earth's New AI Lets Anyone Fabricate Completely Bullshit Satellite Images

Google Earth's new generative AI feature allows anyone to conjure fake satellite imagery with a single sentence — refugees at the border, nuclear plants in Iran, crash scenes in Amsterdam. The implications for geopolitical disinformation are immediate and severe. This is the "photos are evidence" assumption dying in real time.

SIMON WILLISON

Stateless MCP Has Recaptured My Interest

MCP 2.0 (formally the 2026-07-28 spec) dropped this week and Willison calls it the most significant change to the Model Context Protocol since launch. Stateless architecture means agents can swap underlying harnesses — Claude, Codex, Goose — without losing context. The plumbing of the agentic web just got standardized.

DeepSeek V4 Flash: 304B Parameters, Punching Well Above Its Weight

DeepSeek dropped another open-weight model with "substantially enhanced agentic capabilities" — 304 billion parameters, available on Hugging Face at 167GB. Artificial Analysis benchmarks suggest it outperforms its size class by a significant margin. The open-weight arms race is not slowing down.

Company Offering Printed Books to Train AI Stops After 404 Media Coverage

ISBNdb quietly marketed a service to sell scanned books for AI training data, then pulled all webpages and claimed the whole thing was a "test of market interest" after 404 Media published. The denial is barely plausible — but the reporting worked.

03
Commentary & Synthesis

The Thread

The story underneath all the stories this week is that the infrastructure layer of AI just quietly matured. Stateless MCP 2.0 means agents can now travel between execution environments without losing their context. DeepSeek keeps shipping open-weight models that rival proprietary ones. The "moat is the model" thesis is crumbling — the moat is increasingly deployment, taste, and the ability to actually run the stack.
"Every company can now buy the same frontier intelligence, so the real advantage moves to deployment."
Meanwhile Google Earth's fake satellite image problem is a preview of a nastier fight ahead. Disinformation historically required nation-state resources or at least a talented Photoshop operator. One sentence in a consumer product now produces plausible geopolitical fabrications. The same week open-weight models got cheaper and agents got more portable, the verification problem got substantially harder. These aren't unrelated trends. The ISBNdb story is a small but telling data point: the market for training data is so hot that companies are running quiet viability tests on legally questionable supply, then retreating when the light gets turned on. Expect more of this, not less. The demand for data is structural and the supply of clean, licensed material is genuinely constrained. Someone will find a way to close that gap that nobody likes.
