Hack Reveals Suno AI Music Generator Scraped YouTube, Deezer, and Genius

Leaked source code confirms Suno systematically scraped decades of music and podcasts from YouTube, Deezer, and Genius to train its AI. This is the clearest evidentiary smoking gun yet in the AI training data wars — and it lands right as copyright litigation heats up across the industry.

SIMON WILLISON

xAI's Grok CLI Goes Open Source After Privacy Backlash

xAI open-sourced its Grok CLI coding agent after users discovered it could silently upload entire local directories to xAI's Google Cloud buckets. The transparency move came under pressure — but the code is now public and written in Rust, which has the open-source crowd excited.

SIMON WILLISON

Researcher Tricks Claude Into Leaking User Secrets via Web Fetch

Ayush Paul found a hole in Claude's web_fetch tool that allows prompt injection attacks to exfiltrate user data — despite Anthropic's careful design to prevent exactly this. Another reminder that agentic AI tools with real-world access expand the attack surface dramatically.

03
Commentary & Synthesis

The Thread

Three stories today point at the same structural crack in the AI industry's foundation: the inputs are dirty, the tools are leaky, and nobody has figured out liability yet. Suno's scraped training data, Claude's exfiltration vulnerability, and xAI's accidental directory-upload bug all share a common thread — companies shipping fast and hardening later, hoping legal and security consequences arrive slowly.

The attack surface of agentic AI isn't a bug to be patched. It's a design choice being made at scale, right now, with real user data.
The more interesting tension is between the open-source surge and the trust deficit it creates. Grok CLI going open-source after a privacy scandal is a good outcome — but it only happened because users caught the problem. Inkling dropping as a surprise open model, Hermes Agent integrations spreading across harnesses, the Nous Research hackathon winners building agent custody tools — the open ecosystem is moving fast and generating genuine innovation. But "open" doesn't automatically mean "safe" or "audited."

Meanwhile, the creative economy is getting repriced in real time. Vibecoded game clones in hours. Studio-quality video with a laptop. AI IQ scores clearing 136. The floor for entry-level creative and technical work is evaporating faster than any policy, union contract, or business model can adapt. Mollick's frustration with "front-end" as a label for human creativity is right — but the harder problem is that the market doesn't care what we call it.
