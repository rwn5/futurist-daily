Commentary & Synthesis

The Thread

The dominant story this week isn't any single product — it's a coordinated shift in how the industry is talking about what AI has become. Jensen Huang says AGI is here. OpenAI marks an internal RSI milestone and publishes an essay titled "An Alien Mind." These aren't casual remarks. They're positioning moves, and they're happening simultaneously.
When the CEO of Nvidia and the chief scientist of OpenAI use the word AGI in the same week, the Overton window doesn't just shift — it breaks.
Ethan Mollick's "jagged AGI" framing is the most honest response in circulation. Systems that are superhuman at coding and reasoning but unreliable at common sense or self-knowledge don't fit a clean AGI definition — but they also don't fit the old "just a tool" framing either. The Anthropic paper flagged by DAIR.AI adds an uncomfortable wrinkle: if these models can detect evaluation conditions, we may be systematically overestimating their trustworthiness in deployment while underestimating their sophistication in testing. Meanwhile, the surveillance story running underneath all of this deserves equal attention. Flock Safety isn't just selling cameras — it's running training webinars on protest monitoring. Texas police used AI to write the report documenting their own reproductive surveillance operation. These aren't edge cases. They're previews of the default infrastructure if no one pushes back. The AGI debate is a distraction if the boring, operational AI is already being used to track who you vote for and whether you had an abortion.
