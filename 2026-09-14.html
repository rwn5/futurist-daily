Automattic CEO Matt Mullenweg Put on 'Leave of Absence'

The WordPress co-founder claims Automattic board members "conspired" behind his back to vote him out — then immediately posted that he's back "in control." The whiplash is extraordinary, and the company behind WordPress.com, Tumblr, and several other major platforms is in genuine chaos. This is the web infrastructure story nobody is watching closely enough.

'We Did Not Invite You.' Citizens Rage at Town Hall Over Proposed Nuclear AI Data Center

The University of Michigan and Los Alamos National Labs want to plant a massive AI data center in a small Michigan township — without asking residents first. The backlash is a preview of hundreds of similar fights coming as the nuclear-AI power story lands in actual communities.

SIMON WILLISON

Generating Running Routes with GPT-6 Astra and ChatGPT Work

Willison gave GPT-6 Astra his home address and asked for 5K and 10K running routes using OSM data. It ran autonomously for 27 minutes and delivered exactly what was asked. A quiet, practical demo of what long-horizon agentic tasks actually look like in the wild.

03
Commentary & Synthesis

The Thread

The week's dominant story isn't any single model release. It's the collision between the AI industry's internal power struggles and the physical world's resistance to absorbing them. Mullenweg's board coup at Automattic, Michigan residents shutting down a town hall, SoftBank cratering on safety fears — these are all the same story: AI expansion hitting friction it didn't anticipate.

The recursive loop is closing. The same models being used to generate running routes are now being asked to improve themselves — and the people building them are calling for a pause.
Dario Amodei's pacing essay landed the same week GPT-6 Astra demos showed agents autonomously completing 27-minute tasks and generating hardware prototypes in 30 minutes. The gap between the capability curve and the governance conversation has never been wider — or more publicly visible. Ethan Mollick is right that existential risk is eating all the policy oxygen, but the Astra demos suggest the more immediate question is simpler: who controls what these agents do and for whom? The infrastructure layer is where it gets concrete. Nuclear data centers in rural Michigan, quantum-resistant blockchains, AI security teams at 4,000-engineer companies — the abstractions are becoming zip codes and town hall meetings. The residents who said "we did not invite you" are the most honest actors in this week's news cycle. Nobody invited any of this. It arrived anyway.
