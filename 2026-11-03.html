Vibe-Coded AO3 Clone Buried by 2,000 Takedown Notices in One Day

A vibe-coded site scraping fanfiction from Archive of Our Own and reselling it as AI-generated audiobooks got obliterated by a coordinated DMCA blitz from the very authors it exploited. ao3.life is now offline. This is what organized creator resistance to AI scraping actually looks like in practice.

SIMON WILLISON

A Background Sound in Your Videos Can Pinpoint Your Exact Location

OSINT researcher Colsto showed you can extract near-inaudible airplane noise from a video, map its Doppler curve, and cross-reference against ADS-B flight data to geolocate the recording. Terrifying, brilliant, and almost certainly already in use by state actors.

ALL-IN PODCAST

Anthropic S-1 Drops: Greatest Business Ever? Plus Google's $500B Wipeout

The All-In crew dissects Anthropic's IPO filing, debating whether its margins and moat make it the greatest business ever built — or a doomer bet dressed in revenue. Separately, Google shed $500 billion in market cap in a single session, its worst day on record.

Why Frontier Models Still Can't Parse a PDF

Reducto CEO Adit Abraham makes the case that AI agents are blocked not by reasoning limits but by document format chaos — PDFs, scans, spreadsheets. The "document layer" is a sleeper infrastructure problem with massive stakes in healthcare, legal, and finance.

The FBI named the North Korean state-linked group TraderTraitor in the largest crypto hack in history. Pyongyang denied involvement. Binance has already burned through half its SAFU emergency fund — roughly $580 million — responding to the fallout.

From the Feed

X / Twitter Signal

@KobeissiLetter

The Kobeissi Letter flagged a staggering number: Alphabet, Meta, Microsoft, Amazon, and Oracle have now committed to a record $3.3 trillion in future capex. That's not spending — that's a bet the size of Germany's GDP that the AI infrastructure buildout is real and durable. Meanwhile C&I bank lending is up 11.4% YoY, much of it flowing into that same infrastructure. The money is not hedging.

@karpathy

Andrej Karpathy built HackerTube over the weekend — a YouTube filter trained on 43 million Hacker News comments spanning 20 years, surfacing only the 6,454 videos the HN community actually valued. It's a quiet but pointed critique: recommendation algorithms optimize for engagement, not quality. One person, one weekend, one Claude session fixed that for himself.

@DAIR_AI

Stanford researchers improved frozen robot policies from 9.7% to 43.3% success on unseen environments — without retraining the underlying model — using evolved harnesses. That's a 4x jump in generalization by wrapping, not replacing. The implication: the next wave of robotics gains might come from scaffolding, not model improvements.

03
Commentary & Synthesis

The Thread

Two stories today share the same skeleton: people building extractive products on top of other people's work, and the backlash arriving faster than anyone expected. ao3.life lasted roughly 24 hours against an organized fanfic community. The $6.4 billion Binance hack is forcing the exchange to drain its own safety reserves. Speed of exploitation is up; so is speed of consequence.

The most dangerous assumption in AI right now is that scraping is the same as permission.
The $3.3 trillion capex commitment from Big Tech is the real signal hiding in plain sight. That number isn't a forecast — it's already disclosed, contractually bound future spending. When five companies stake that much on a single technology wave, they've made retrenchment nearly impossible. The AI infrastructure bet is now too large to unwind quietly, which means the pressure to show returns will become intense and visible within 18 months. Karpathy's HackerTube hack is a small thing that points at a big idea: the curation layer is broken, and AI can fix it — but only if someone bothers to apply taste. The same week we learn frontier models still can't reliably parse a PDF, a guy with a weekend and Claude built a better YouTube. The bottleneck in AI isn't always the model. Sometimes it's just who decides to aim it at the right problem.
