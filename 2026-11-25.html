The Thread

@milesdeutscher

Miles Deutscher nailed the AI productivity paradox in one line: "AI was supposed to give me my time back. Instead, it took a task that used to take 5 hours and made it take 20 minutes, so now I just do 30 of them." This is the efficiency treadmill in real time — output expands to fill the time saved, and the floor on human effort just keeps dropping.

@KobeissiLetter

President Trump floated the idea of the US government taking an equity stake in Nvidia — "It's probably not too late," he said. With NVDA down over 5% in premarket and SoftBank dropping 10% in Asian markets, the timing is either opportunistic or desperate. Either way, state capitalism is knocking on the door of the AI chip stack.

@emollick

Ethan Mollick flagged what may be the week's most significant scientific event almost in passing: an AI agent appears to have produced a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis, with researchers saying "there's virtually no doubt the proof is correct." If this holds up, it's not a headline — it's a before-and-after moment for mathematics and AI credibility alike.

@rasbt

Sebastian Raschka reframed the entire frontier model race in one tweet: "Whoever gets their hands on the most battle-tested enterprise code… this is how the ChatGPT vs Claude vs Gemini vs Muse vs Grok race is going to be decided." It's not about benchmarks. It's about which model gets embedded deepest into production systems before the others notice.
