ICE to Pay Thomson Reuters $125 Million to Find 'Voter Fraud'

ICE is contracting Thomson Reuters for access to personal data including Social Security numbers and ethnicity, ostensibly to investigate "voter fraud" and immigration fraud. The scale and specificity of the data grab — targeting ethnicity — raises serious civil liberties flags. This is surveillance infrastructure being quietly contracted out to a private data broker.

SIMON WILLISON

AI Mania Is Eviscerating Global Decision-Making

Consultant Nik Suresh documents how AI hype is warping corporate strategy at the highest levels — boards demanding AI pivots with no coherent plan, executives cosplaying transformation. Willison flags it as must-read for anyone navigating enterprise AI reality versus theater.

SIMON WILLISON

Quoting Sam Altman on Open Source Strategy

A leaked internal discussion reveals OpenAI was debating releasing a GPT-3-class model that runs locally on consumer hardware — before competitors forced their hand. The "before" is doing a lot of work in that sentence; it confirms open source was always a competitive defense, not a philosophy.

Commentary & Synthesis

The Thread

The market data today tells a specific story: equities down, VIX spiking over 12%, oil above $90, and Treasury yields at two-decade highs — all while Bitcoin quietly ticks up nearly 1%. The correlation breakdown between crypto and tech equities is worth watching. Chinese AI releases shook confidence in US AI dominance enough to drag the Nasdaq 1.4%, but Bitcoin found a floor. That's a data point, not a trend, but it's notable.
"78% of people have never used AI. The revolution is still in the lobby."
The real story threading through today's feed is the gap between AI as experienced by insiders and AI as it actually exists in the world. Ethan Mollick watches his predictions arrive early. Teknium ships agent introspection. a16z funds self-improving systems. Meanwhile Miles Deutscher's stat hangs in the air: the overwhelming majority of humans have never typed a prompt. The discourse about AI transforming everything is largely a conversation happening among the 0.15%. That gap is both an opportunity and a warning. The ICE-Thomson Reuters contract is a reminder that the institutions most aggressively deploying AI at scale right now are governments and law enforcement — not necessarily the builders writing the breathless threads about agentic futures. The leaked Altman discussion about releasing a local GPT-3-class model "before competitors forced their hand" confirms what most suspected: open source at OpenAI was always a defensive maneuver, not a mission. Transparency as strategy rather than principle ages poorly when the receipts surface.
