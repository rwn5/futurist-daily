How Researchers Hijacked ChatGPT Accounts Through Canvas

Parameter Labs disclosed an exploit in ChatGPT's "artifacts" pattern — formerly called Canvas — that allowed full account takeover. Simon Willison flagged it as a significant find. Proton's CEO Cancels AI Re-Interviews After Investigation

Following a 404 Media exposé, Proton CEO Andy Yen scrapped plans to use AI to re-evaluate existing employees — a practice that had created internal fear at a company selling itself on trust and privacy. The optics couldn't be worse for a privacy-first brand.

SIMON WILLISON

Kyle Kingsbury on LLMs Eroding Trust in Software

Jepsen founder Kyle Kingsbury argues that LLMs are counterfeiting the signals engineers rely on to judge software maturity — good docs, coherent APIs, consistent behavior. The concern isn't just hallucinations; it's that trustworthy-looking software is now cheap to fake.

Why Frontier Models Still Can't Parse a PDF

Reducto CEO Adit Abraham makes the case that the document layer — PDFs, scans, spreadsheets — remains the unsexy bottleneck blocking AI agents from doing real work in healthcare, legal, and finance. Reasoning is solved; ingestion isn't.

Agentic shopping isn't a future scenario — it's already outperforming every other acquisition channel, which means whoever controls the AI shopping layer controls the transaction.

03
Commentary & Synthesis

The Thread

Two stories this week share a skeleton: the systems we built trust on are being quietly hollowed out. Parameter Labs showed that ChatGPT's own interactive canvas could be turned into an account-takeover weapon. Kyle Kingsbury warned that LLMs now make it trivially cheap to produce software that

looks
mature — good docs, clean APIs — while being rotten underneath. These aren't separate bugs. They're the same bug at different layers of the stack.

"The signals we used to rely on as markers of a mature system are now easier to counterfeit." The Proton story is the week's clearest case study in what happens when an AI-first mandate collides with brand identity. A privacy company using AI to secretly re-evaluate its own employees is not just an HR scandal — it's an existential contradiction. Yen canceled the program after press exposure, but the damage is already priced in: if you sell trust, you cannot secretly surveil the people building the product.

Meanwhile, Karpathy's HackerTube hack and a16z's conversion data point in the same direction from opposite ends. AI is getting better at filtering for what actually matters — whether that's 20 years of tech commentary or a buyer ready to transact. The platforms optimizing for engagement are going to lose ground to systems optimizing for signal. That's a slow landslide, until suddenly it isn't.
