03
Commentary & Synthesis

The Thread

Two stories today share the same skeleton: AI is being embedded into consequential processes faster than anyone built guardrails for. Texas police didn't just use Flock to surveil a woman seeking abortion care — they used a second AI to write the report documenting it. That's not a cautionary hypothetical. It happened, it was routine, and it was only visible because someone filed a records request.
"The incident shows how quickly police departments have come to use AI surveillance tools even in critically sensitive cases."
Meanwhile, Gemini 3.8 Flash dropped as the second major model upgrade in a month, and DAIR.AI is surfacing credible speculation that recent Flash improvements are early outputs of a recursive self-improvement loop. Whether or not that's precisely true, the release cadence alone is doing something to the industry's nervous system — everyone is benchmarking, re-evaluating, and shipping faster than their security or policy teams can follow. The a16z enshittification denial piece belongs in this context too. The firm that profits from AI platform disruption is arguing the platforms people want to escape are actually great. It's not an intellectual argument — it's a positioning document. When the same week brings self-improving models, AI-drafted police surveillance reports, and disguised surveillance cameras, the claim that everything is fine online deserves more skepticism than it's getting.
