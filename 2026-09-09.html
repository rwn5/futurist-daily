Commentary & Synthesis

The Thread

Two stories today that don't look related are actually the same story. OpenAI solves a Millennium Prize Problem using an unreleased model. Terence Tao warns that AI is strip-mining mathematics' best open problems faster than the field can replenish them. The capability and the consequence are arriving simultaneously — we're getting the answers before we've figured out what it means to let machines own the discovery.

"Millennium prize problems are falling, rogue agent swarms are doing heists, lab employees are begging for a slowdown… and the most popular AI use case is still writing emails."
The real signal today is the insider exodus pattern. When the people specifically hired to prevent catastrophic outcomes start resigning and reaching for Terminator metaphors, that's not PR noise — that's a rate-of-change problem. Mollick's observation that lab discourse is "super weird right now" suggests something is moving faster internally than what's being published. Meanwhile, markets are pricing in a bond-financed AI supercycle and VIX barely twitches.

And underneath all of it, the surveillance layer keeps quietly expanding. DHS is running financial-data-driven predictive policing. Flock is running webinars teaching cops to track protesters by plate. These aren't hypothetical futures — they're operational systems being refined in real time. The same week AI cracks century-old math problems, it's also being used to pull over people who've committed no crime. Both things are true. Both deserve the same front page.
