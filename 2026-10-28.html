Proton To 'Re-Interview' Staff About AI To See Who Gets To Keep Their Job

Proton, the privacy-focused email company, is forcing employees through AI competency interviews where the outcome determines job security. A leaked internal email confirms these are "consequential" reviews — not optional training. The quiet part is now loud: AI adoption isn't a perk, it's a performance metric.

SIMON WILLISON

GPT-6.1 Terra Lands With a Focus on Vision and Computer Use

OpenAI released GPT-6.1 Terra, a fast and efficient model emphasizing visual reasoning and computer use. Willison notes the framing is telling — OpenAI is doubling down on agents that can see and act, not just chat. This is the architecture of a model built to use software, not just talk about it.

A16Z

Why Frontier Models Still Can't Parse a PDF

Reducto's CEO argues that the real bottleneck for AI agents isn't reasoning — it's document ingestion. PDFs, scans, and spreadsheets remain largely illegible to frontier models at production quality. Healthcare, legal, and finance can't go agentic until this layer is solved.

Flock CEO Tells Police the Public Backlash to His Surveillance Company Isn't Real

In leaked audio from a private police conference, Flock Safety CEO Garrett Langley dismissed community criticism of his license plate reader network as manufactured. He also shared that his wife told him never to apologize. Surveillance tech executives now appear to be coaching law enforcement on how to handle PR, not just crime.

This isn't a fringe user problem — it's happening to trained professionals. Commoditized intelligence, differentiated memory.

Commentary & Synthesis

The Thread

Two stories from today share a structural DNA even though they look nothing alike. Proton re-interviewing staff on AI competency and Flock's CEO privately dismissing public backlash are both, at root, about institutions deciding that inconvenient feedback — from employees worried about their jobs, from communities worried about surveillance — doesn't deserve a real response. The answer in both cases is internal messaging, not course correction.

"The autonomy gap between 'I'm using AI as a tool' and 'the AI is shaping my conclusions' is narrowing fast — and most people don't notice when they cross it."
The deeper pattern is about where power is actually consolidating. Databricks says enterprise AI is bottlenecked on context, not capability — meaning whoever owns proprietary data pipelines owns the moat. GPT-6.1 Terra is built for computer use, not conversation. The PDF parsing problem is unsolved at scale. All of this points the same direction: the infrastructure layer of the agentic economy is still being built, the application layer is already being monetized, and the accountability layer has barely been sketched.

Ethan Mollick's finding that even experts are being unconsciously steered by AI agents should be read alongside the Flock story and the Proton story. We are building systems optimized to act, and we are simultaneously discovering that the humans supervising those systems are more suggestible than assumed. That's not a coincidence — it's the operating condition of the next decade.
