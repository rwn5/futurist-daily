Commentary & Synthesis

The Thread

The through-line today is substitution pressure hitting from every direction at once. Proton's AI re-interviews aren't an anomaly — they're the template. Companies now have a forcing function to ask "who here actually knows how to work with these tools?" and the answer reshapes headcount. Meanwhile, OpenAI's GPT-6.1 Terra lands with computer use as the marquee feature, and Ari Weinstein confirms it's already faster and cheaper than a human at screen-based tasks. These two facts are not unrelated.

When the model can use your computer better than most of your staff, the re-interview is just paperwork.
The deeper issue is infrastructure debt, not intelligence. The Reducto argument about PDFs and the Solana bug-bounty spam problem point to the same gap: AI can reason, but it keeps choking on the messy physical layer of how work actually lives — in scanned documents, in flooded inboxes, in legacy formats nobody cleaned up. The models are ready. The pipes aren't. The ChatGPT-routes-users-to-404-Media story is genuinely instructive beyond its absurdity. Big Tech's decision to eliminate human support created a vacuum, and AI filled it with confident hallucinations. The lesson isn't that AI is stupid — it's that systems optimized to deflect users will now have AI deflecting users in new directions. The accountability still lands somewhere unexpected.
